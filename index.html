Statistics · Machine learning · Explainable AI

Statistics and explainable machine learning for survey research — in your browser.

SaNaML screens your data for careless responders, runs the classical tests with plain-language results, plans your sample size, and compares and explains machine learning models — with every cut-off traced to a published source and a Word report for every result. What it does

Eight parts, from your data file to your Word report

Start here

Data and construct scores

Open a CSV, Excel or SPSS file (or the built-in teaching data), check the variable types, build construct scores with Cronbach’s α, or bring latent variable scores from SaNaPLS.

Open your data →
Recommended first

Data quality screening

Careless responding (longstring, response variability, Mahalanobis distance, even–odd consistency, response time), outliers, missing-data patterns and Little’s MCAR test. You decide whom to exclude.

Screen your data →
Common statistical analysis

Tests with plain-language results

Descriptives, t-tests, one-way, factorial and repeated-measures ANOVA, ANCOVA, MANOVA, non-parametric tests, chi-square, Fisher and McNemar tests, correlation, linear and logistic regression, and a guide that helps you choose.

Open the analyses →
No data needed

Power analysis and sample size

A priori, post hoc and sensitivity analyses for regression, t-tests, ANOVA, chi-square and correlation, and the inverse square root method for PLS-SEM, with a sentence ready for your proposal.

Plan your sample →
Explainable AI

Machine learning

Compare linear models, elastic net, decision trees, random forest, XGBoost and LightGBM on respondents they never saw (nested cross-validation), then explain the best model with SHAP, permutation importance and accumulated local effects, and check calibration and prediction intervals.

Predict and explain →
For SaNaPLS users

PLS-SEM link

Benchmark your structural model against machine learning, test curved effects and interactions, check paths with double machine learning, and draw importance–performance maps from a SaNaPLS results file.

Test your PLS-SEM model →
For SaNaPLS users

Heterogeneity

Segmentation trees of the structural model, causal forests for one path, and latent profile analysis: find groups of respondents for whom the model works differently.

Find groups →
Every result

Word report

Every result has a ready APA-style sentence, the checks with their rules and sources, and numbered tables. Collect the results you want and download them as one Word document.

Open the report →

Why SaNaML

Guidance at every step, and honest machine learning

Nothing to install

Runs in any modern browser on Windows, Mac or Linux. Machine learning runs in your browser too.

Your data stays yours

Every calculation runs on your computer. Nothing is uploaded to a server.

Sources for every rule

Each check states its rule and the publication it comes from, and the Word report lists them all.

Tested on unseen respondents

Models are judged by nested cross-validation, with a guard against leakage, so the results hold up.

  • Example files used in the manual: careless_demo.csv (data quality screening), utaut_items_demo.csv and utaut.sanahandoff (PLS-SEM link and heterogeneity). The teaching data are built in: Data → Try the teaching data.
